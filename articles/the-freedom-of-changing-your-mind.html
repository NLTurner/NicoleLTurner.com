The Freedom of Changing Your Mind

There was a time when I thought changing my mind meant I'd been wrong.

Now I think it often means I've learned something.

We put a surprising amount of pressure on ourselves to be consistent.

We make a decision.

We announce it.

We tell other people.

Then, somewhere along the way, we realize it no longer fits.

But instead of changing direction, we hesitate.

Not because changing direction is the wrong decision.

Because we're worried someone will notice we changed our minds.

I've changed my mind about careers.

About what success looks like.

About how I spend my weekends.

About what deserves my time.

About things I once thought were important.

None of those changes happened overnight.

They happened because life has a way of teaching us.

Experience has a way of reshaping us.

And sometimes the wisest thing we can do is give ourselves permission to become someone our younger selves couldn't have imagined.

Changing your mind doesn't erase who you were.

It simply makes room for who you're becoming.

Maybe consistency isn't always about continuing to believe what you've always believed.

Maybe sometimes it's about being willing to let what you've learned change you.

When's the last time you changed your mind about something important?
